How does a signal get there?

Two ways a signal travels

Both stages use the same pieces: a releasing cell, signal molecules, receptors on a target cell. Watch which gradient lights up at each step.

Paracrine: a neuron to a nearby cell

Distance: very short

  1. Release
  2. Diffuse across the gap
  3. Bind receptors
  4. Target responds
Concentration gradientIn use
Pressure gradientNot used

What to notice: the signal never enters the blood. Diffusion down its concentration gradient is the only thing moving it.

Endocrine: a gland cell to a distant cell

Distance: anywhere blood goes

  1. Release
  2. Into the capillary
  3. Carried by blood
  4. Out of the capillary
  5. Bind receptors
  6. Target responds
Concentration gradientIn use
Pressure gradientIn use
The lower cell has no receptor, so the hormone reaches it with no effect.

What to notice: diffusion moves the hormone into and out of the blood, but blood pressure carries it the long distance in between.
